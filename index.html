Kerbside waste in Victoria's 79 councils

Where Victoria's rubbish goes

The average Victorian household puts out 16.2 kg of kerbside waste a week. In 2023–24 the state kept 52.7% of it out of landfill, up from 29.7% in 2001–02.

Councils range from 74% in Alpine Shire to 16% in West Wimmera. This page follows that gap down to the bins each council hands out.

Before you start

Four bins and one number

Diversion is the share of a council's kerbside waste kept out of landfill. Councils report it every year, and it is the measure this page uses most.

Red lid: garbage

Everything that goes to landfill. Collected weekly almost everywhere.

1.11 Mt in 2023–24

Lime lid: food and garden

Garden clippings, and in 55 councils food scraps too (FOGO). Composted.

718 kt in 2023–24

Yellow lid: recycling

Paper, plastics and metals, sorted at a materials recovery facility (MRF).

503 kt in 2023–24

Purple lid: glass

Kept apart so it does not shatter through the yellow bin. 16 councils.

16 kt in 2023–24

These four colours mean the same stream on every chart. They are not the lid colours: a red lid beside a green one is the pair colour-blind readers confuse most, so the charts use a colour-blind-safe set and the page names the lids in words.

Why

Councils publish their waste figures one council at a time, in spreadsheets. Side by side they show a gap in service that residents have no easy way to see.

Who

Any Victorian who puts a bin out. I assume no statistics background and define each term where it first appears.

What

Five government datasets: 23 years of kerbside tonnages and five years of costs for all 79 councils, council boundaries, 664 waste facilities and the 2021 census disadvantage index.

How

Maps where the pattern is about place. One dot per council where it is about spread, so you can count the councils behind each claim. Findings are written into each chart title.

Wyndham buries as much as the ten lowest-scoring councils combined

The ten councils with the lowest diversion handle 3.6% of Victoria's kerbside waste and send about 61,300 tonnes to landfill. Wyndham, at 34%, sends 61,400 tonnes on its own.

The map above gives West Wimmera more ink than all of inner Melbourne. Weighted by tonnes, the councils that decide the state's total are the big outer suburbs: Wyndham, Hume, Brimbank and Melton all keep less than 41% out of landfill.

The share lost more than tripled in eight years

In 2015–16, 5.3% of the yellow bin was collected but not recovered. In 2023–24 it was 18.7%, about 94,000 tonnes.

The source counts contamination and sorting losses together and cannot split them. Several things changed in the recycling industry over these years, and I have not tried to pin the rise on any one of them.

03The bins

A green bin is worth 23 points

Councils with a food and garden bin keep a median 52.6% out of landfill. The 13 without one keep 29.6%. No other measure in this data separates councils by that much.

The third bin moves the result; the fourth does not

A heatmap. Each cell counts the councils that offer those bins and keep that share of their waste out of landfill, in five-point bands; darker cells hold more councils. The marker on each row is its median. 2024–25.

Local Government Victoria, LGPRF indicator WC5, and Recycling Victoria, VLGAS release 2025.03

Three councils added glass before a green bin

A green bin on top of the two basic bins lifts the median from 30.6% to 53.8%. A glass bin on top of that leaves it at 52.4%.

Hindmarsh, Yarra and Yarriambiack run a glass bin but no green bin. Hindmarsh and Yarriambiack rank 77th and 78th of 79. Three councils are too few to call a pattern, so the chart names each of them.

The western shires are a long truck ride from a plant

Food and garden waste has to go to an organics processing site. Councils without a green bin usually point to the cost of getting it there.

Distance fits the west, not the whole list

Ten of the 13 are about 30 km or more from a site, more than twice the 13 km of the median council that has a green bin. Glenelg is 103 km away, Hindmarsh 86 km and West Wimmera 80 km.

Yarra is 12 km from a site, Murrindindi 15 km and Mansfield 23 km, so distance does not explain those three. Yarra's nearest site is also a grease-trap service, which would not take a household's food scraps, so its real trip is longer than the map shows.

05What it costs

The councils that bury the most also pay the most

A garbage bin costs a council a median $138 a year to service, and a recycling bin $72. Across the 79 councils, the cheaper the garbage bin, the more the council keeps out of landfill.

The cheapest quarter keeps 66% out of landfill; the dearest keeps 39%

One row per council, sorted by what a garbage bin costs. The solid dot is the garbage bin, the hollow dot the recycling bin, and the bar between them is coloured by diversion. Bands mark quarters of the list. 2024–25.

Local Government Victoria, LGPRF indicators WC3 and WC5, 2024–25. Cost is each council's reported direct cost per kerbside bin, per year.

Cost and diversion move together; the data cannot say which drives which

Melbourne City is the outlier at $476 a garbage bin, for city-centre collections, and keeps 28% out of landfill. The recycling bin is the cheaper of the two in 75 of the 79 councils.

Small remote councils pay more per bin and are far from processing at the same time. Both show up in this chart, so it cannot separate them. It does raise the next question: whether this is about money.

06Who gets what

Better-off councils divert a little more; poorer ones are not missing out on bins

If results follow money, the worry is that poorer areas miss out on services. The data supports the first half of that and not the second.

07What changed

Garbage tonnes are where they were in 2002

Kerbside collection grew from 1.57 to 2.35 million tonnes between 2001–02 and 2023–24. Almost none of that growth went into the red-lid bin.

Every stream grew except garbage

Food and garden passed recycling in 2020–21

Garbage was 1.10 million tonnes in 2001–02 and 1.11 million in 2023–24, while the state added two million people. Food and garden went from 111,000 to 718,000 tonnes.

The first separate glass bins show up in 2019–20.

Most of the change was existing green bins taking food

3 → 55

Over the same years only 14 councils gained a green bin they had not had before, taking the total from 52 to 66.

The state wants every council on four bins by 2030. Thirteen still have two or three, and none of them has the green one.

Where that leaves it

Thirteen councils are one bin away

Metropolitan and regional councils keep the same share of their waste out of landfill. The gap the data does show is narrower: 13 councils, most of them small and rural, have no green bin and keep a median 29.6% out of landfill against 52.6% for the rest.

Ten of them are 30 km or more from an organics site. Yarra, Murrindindi and Mansfield are not. Three have already paid for a glass bin.

Victoria has set 2030 for four bins everywhere. For these 13 councils, the green bin is the one that would move the number.

Notes on the data

  • Diversion is each council's own reported figure under the performance reporting framework. Where I needed years before 2020–21, I computed it from tonnages instead; the two agree closely (r = 0.95).
  • "No green bin" means the 13 councils with no kerbside organics collection. Ten of them have only two bins; the other three run a glass bin.
  • Distances to processing sites are straight-line from each council's geographic centre, which understates the road trip.
  • Victoria's container deposit scheme began in November 2023, partway through the latest year of data, so I have not treated it as a measured effect.
  • Stream and service colours are based on the Okabe-Ito set and checked under deuteranopia, protanopia and tritanopia. Quantities use ColorBrewer single-hue ramps, darker meaning more.

Use of generative AI

I used generative AI (Claude) to find and check the data sources, debug Vega-Lite specifications, compute the Dorling cartogram positions, review the page's layout and colour against the unit's design principles, and copy-edit the text.
